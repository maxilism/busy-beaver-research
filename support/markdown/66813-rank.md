# Multiplicative independence in BB(6) machine 66813

maxilism · 25 November 2026

The [Busy Beaver Explorer analysis of machine 66813][machine] follows part of its blank-tape run using a sequence whose first terms are $22,89,284,915,2868$. **All 1,567 terms used in level 1 are multiplicatively independent.** That is, two products of these terms are equal only if each term occurs equally often on both sides. The rank of a list is the largest number of multiplicatively independent terms we can select from it.

### The first 1,567 numbers

Write the current number as $x=10a+d$, where $d$ is its last decimal digit. Group the digits into pairs: $0,1$ give index $j=0$, $2,3$ give $j=1$, and so on. The [digit rule][code] says to multiply $a$ by 31, then add entry $j$ of $(15,27,47,35,36)$, counting from zero. For example, $22$ gives $31\cdot2+27=89$. Starting at $22$, the first level has a budget of 6,801 tape blocks. An update costs entry $j$ of $(4,5,8,3,2)$. It takes [1,566 updates][machine] to use all 6,801 blocks.

Our [short Python check](../verification/bb-6-2/66813/check.py) finds that each of the 1,567 terms has a prime factor absent from every earlier term. For example, the first three terms introduce $11$, $89$ and $71$ respectively. The check needs no factorization. Let $P$ be the product of the earlier terms. Starting with the current term, divide out its greatest common divisor with $P$ until the number left is coprime to $P$. For every term the number left is greater than one, so it has a new prime factor. Now suppose two products are equal and take the last term whose exponents differ. Its new prime has unequal exponents on the two sides, a contradiction.

### Beyond the first level

The same digit rule has the form $10x'=31x+e$. Indeed $e=10K_j-31d$, where $K_j$ is the entry added above. Checking the ten digits gives $81\leq e\leq346$. [Corollary 1.2 of *Multiplicative rank along expanding affine paths of integers*][rank] applies directly with $p=31$, $q=10$ and $B=346$: for every $N\geq1$, at least $cN/\sqrt{\log(2N)}$ of the first $N+1$ terms are multiplicatively independent, for a constant $c>0$ independent of $N$.

Level 2 of the blank-tape run starts the same digit sequence from 22 with a different budget. The machine follows it only until too few tape blocks remain to pay for the next update. The rank bound applies however far the arithmetic sequence is continued. It does not tell us which digit the machine stops on in level 2 or whether it eventually halts.
