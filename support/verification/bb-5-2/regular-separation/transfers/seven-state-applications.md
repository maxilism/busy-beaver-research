25 November 2026 · Local research note

The ordinary separation results apply to their actual seven-state configuration languages. ## What a returning detour preserves

The two helpers, entered from a core instruction, restore the shadow tape, state and head on return. Toggles always return. Scans of ones encounter a zero on every finite-support tape and return. In either case all shadow orbits and all their eventually halting configurations at core positions are simulated by the seven-state table, starting with the same tape and head. Intersecting a seven-state separator with the regular language of core positions and renaming its states therefore gives a shadow separator. If the original language is inductive, its restriction contains the result of each complete call and so is inductive for the shadow. This proves the negative entries for all total-return detours. [Transfer proof](https://github.com/maxilism/busy-beaver-research/blob/682350182398fa879b9db9fe73a043fe10596b47/results/bb-7-2/restoring-detours.pdf)

In the other direction, any regular ordinary separator of the shadow lifts, even for a scan of zeros that can run forever. Include the accepted shadow words at core states. For each accepted caller add the intermediate words seen on every finite prefix of its helper excursion, stopping before the return to the core. This construction is regular. Each caller test is regular, a toggle has bounded length, and a scan consists of one outward and one returning monotone rewrite, each with a rational prefix relation. Every accepted core word is safe because a halting seven-state run would return from each call and project to a halting shadow run. Each added helper word lies on a real path from such a safe core word. The blank core positions belong to the shadow blank orbit, and this construction includes all intervening helper words. This proves ordinary separation in all 21 A lifts.

## The three zero scans

In base-table coordinates the scan runs forever precisely when its diverted write-and-move sees no one in the direction of travel. The following arguments concern three different seven-state tables, each with just one diverted instruction.

**A, row 7,613,097.** Its diverted instruction is A1, moving right. The two-pump obstruction to inductive separation for A survives. Its blank prefix through time 14 contains no A1. In its remaining blank and pumped paths A1 occurs only in the preamble to a down sweep or an up sweep. In a down sweep the first alternating one being read still has the final block of at least one one on its right. In an up sweep the right scan leaves A on the first of $c-1$ ones, with $c\ge3$. The same condition holds on the last, partial up sweep of the proof. All such calls return, so every core path used to pass acceptance from one pumping site to the next and finally to D1 is present. [A-start proof, Table 1 and §5](https://github.com/maxilism/busy-beaver-research/blob/ee693aa3127ca2aaa09a37f22e8b846fa6048914/results/bb-5-2/cubic-helix.pdf)

**E, row 3,192,869.** Its diverted instruction is base B1, moving left. All calls on the E blank return have a one to the left. In the initial 21 steps the B1 calls occur at times 5, 14 and 18, each with a one immediately to its left. In the right-scan tile A0 has just written that one. In an entry's left scan the prefix $f1$ stays on the left until the repeated tile ends. In a down sweep the corresponding prefix is $f11$. The ending $11\overline B$ has two adjacent ones. The special entry starts from $1001\overline B$ and its first B1 has the initial one to its left. Its remaining B1 instructions are in the right-scan tile. These are all possible B1 calls in the return. [Sweep rules](https://github.com/maxilism/busy-beaver-research/blob/ee693aa3127ca2aaa09a37f22e8b846fa6048914/results/bb-5-2/cubic-helix.pdf) · [E return, Lemma 2](../data/cubic-helix-blank-roots.pdf)

The second half of that return visits $Q(1\,0^{k+2},0,k+1)$ for every positive multiple $k$ of three. Take $k<l$ in this sequence and splice the shorter left block to the longer right block. After $k/3$ up sweeps the result is $Q(100,k,l-k+1)$. There are at least four remaining ones, so the common up row applies. It leaves B scanning the final zero of $100$. The two left moves B0 and C0 put D on the one. All B1 calls on these up sweeps occur inside the right-scan tile and have their left delimiter. Hence the hybrid halts in the seven-state machine. An automaton accepting the diagonal words has a repeated cut state before D and accepts a halting hybrid. The same cut can be taken in base coordinates because reflection reverses packed tape cells and preserves regularity.

**B, row 6,978,333.** Its diverted instruction is base A1, moving right. A call on the rightmost one starts an endless zero scan. At a core position write “safe” for a run that avoids D1, including one that eventually starts such a scan. All blank-orbit sweeps return, by the down and up guards given for the A row. The B startup through time 7 contains no A1.

Use the checkpoints and notation of the sibling manuscript. For positive multiples of three $i,j$ with $i\equiv j\pmod6$, every $Q(1\,0^i,0,j)$ is safe for this seven-state table. Equal exponents occur on the blank orbit. If $i>j$, the excess is at least six. The proof of the manuscript's Lemma 3 reaches A1 on the rightmost one at its right-margin turn. If $j>i$, the right-heavy calculation for the B start also reaches A1 on the rightmost one. The first such call on either path diverges in this table. The congruence modulo six excludes the distinct fatal excess of exactly three. [Left-heavy calculation](../data/cubic-helix-blank-roots.pdf) · [Right-heavy guard proof](helix-margin.md)

Build a regular set of safe sweep endpoints containing every blank endpoint. The $T$ rows are regular. In the second half of the B return both $P$ and $Q$ rows are regular after trimming their invisible leading zeros. In the first-half $Q$ row keep $a=c=0$ as its own regular family. In the remaining first-half rows $P(1\,0^a,b,a)$ and $Q(1\,0^a,b,a)$, take $a\ge1$, replace the last exponent by any $c\ge1$ with $c\equiv a\pmod6$, and keep all original finite-state bounds and residues on $a,b$. Each relaxed $P$ reaches the safe $Q(1\,0^{a+b},0,c+b)$ after $b$ downs. Each relaxed $Q$ is reached from that safe diagonal by $b/3$ ups, since $a,b,c$ are positive or zero multiples of three there and the subscripts $a+b,c+b$ are positive. All these down and up calls have the required right delimiter. This proves safety of every endpoint. [Full endpoint rows and normalization](sibling-width.md)

Starting at these endpoints, take up to 18 factors of the published step/scan relation to include the intermediate blank words, and add the finite startup. Restrict each single core step A1 to a one strictly to its right. This is a regular guard. The three repeated tiles contain no A1 and every elementary move in them is an actual core move or returning call. Thus the image is regular and safe and contains the full blank core orbit. Add genuine helper prefixes from those accepted core callers as above. This gives a regular ordinary separator for row 6,978,333.

**Inductive separation for the B zero scan.** Suppose a regular inductive separator exists. In base coordinates its restriction to core positions is regular and is closed along every finite sequence of returning shadow calls. Fix $N$ and a positive multiple of three $d$ so that, in the DFA for this restriction, $1^{j+d}$ acts exactly as $1^j$ whenever $j\ge N$. Choose a blank checkpoint $Q(1\,0^a,0,a)$ with $a\ge N$ a positive multiple of three. Pump its right block by any positive multiple $p$ of $d$. The accepted configuration $Q(1\,0^a,0,a+p)$ reaches $Q(1,a,p)$ in $a/3$ ordinary ups. Each A1 encountered has a remaining one to its right.

In the right-heavy calculation the first reduction then gives the D0 successor of $Q(100(10)^1 1,b,p-3)$ with $b=a+1$. Successive reductions use $n=1,4,7,\ldots$ and remaining right exponent $c=p-n-2$. Fix $n$ in this sequence with $n+4\ge N$, then take $p$ large enough that $c\ge6$. The second reduction at this $n$ visits the intermediate word
$$
 Y=Q(1\,0^{n+4},0,n+4)\;0(10)^b1^{c-3}.
$$
Every reduction used so far leaves its final block of $c'-3$ ones uninspected, where $c'\ge c$. Hence every A1 on the path to $Y$ has a one to its right and the seven-state calls return. Inductiveness forces acceptance at $Y$. [Two reductions and their untouched suffixes](helix-margin.md)

Pump the $n+4$ ones of $Y$ by $d$, leaving its suffix fixed. The result is still accepted. Since $n+4\equiv2\pmod3$, exactly $(n+2)/3$ ordinary ups give $Q(100,n+2,d+2)$ with the same suffix. Here $d+2\ge3$, so the common up row leaves B scanning the last zero of $100$. The two moves B0 and C0 halt. The right suffix supplies a one on every diverted A1 step. This contradicts separation. As a finite check on the lifted paths, direct simulation of literal row 6,978,333 for $(a,p)=(3,15),(6,18),(9,21)$ reached all 12 intermediate words $Y$ with $c\ge6$ and halted from each of their second pumps with $d=6$.

## The 29 Finned lifts

The older detour paper gives these 29 distinct rows a context-free exact orbit. The within-sweep obstruction for Finned #3 now rules out a **linear** context-free ordinary separator in every row. For its witnesses $S(d,b,a)$ with $a\ge1$, $b\ge a$, the machine arrives from $C(d+a,b-a)$ within its first pass. The diagonal ground case is reached from blank. The off-diagonal case halts. Every such computation is preserved by the 20 toggles and seven scans of ones. In the remaining two zero scans, rows 14,424,496 and 15,538,865, the established delimiter proof works along the entire return from every $C(n,m)$ for $n,m\ge0$, including the fatal pass. It therefore covers the suffix starting at $S$ and the subsequent returns. Restricting to the core alphabet and the regular witness shape preserves linear context-freeness. The same pumping contradiction follows. [Within-sweep proof](finned.md) · [Zero-scan and orbit proofs](https://github.com/maxilism/busy-beaver-research/blob/682350182398fa879b9db9fe73a043fe10596b47/results/bb-7-2/restoring-detours.pdf)

In fact each exact orbit has a nondeterministic one-counter recognizer using at most **two successive rises and falls** of the counter. Use a nonnegative counter, initially zero and required to end at zero, with $\varepsilon$-moves allowed. In the repair checkpoint $(10)^l1^l\,1^{2r}[C{:}1]1^{r+1}$ the parameters $l,r$ are independent. Guess the division of the middle ones. Check the $l$ equality in one rise and fall, then the $r$ equality in a second rise and fall. The other two checkpoint families use one rise and fall. The published finite collection of rational images of these checkpoints gives the entire seven-state orbit including helper positions. Simulating a transducer on guessed input labels adds only finite control. Under this convention the least counter reversal bound for exact recognition is three: a run starting and ending at zero with at most two reversals has at most one nontrivial rise and fall. A one-turn pushdown language is linear context-free, which the obstruction rules out even for a separator.
